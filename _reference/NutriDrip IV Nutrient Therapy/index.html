        Why IV and not tablets?

        A tablet passes through your stomach, gut and liver before what's left reaches your blood. An IV drip goes straight into a vein.

        Tablets are enough for many people. A doctor may choose IV when absorption is poor, a test shows a deficiency, or nutrients are needed quickly.

          What if it's not right for me?

          If your tests or consult say a drip isn't right for you, you won't be given one.

          The assessment screens for G6PD deficiency, kidney problems, pregnancy, thyroid status, allergies (including fish and shellfish for collagen drips) and the medicines you already take. No drip is given without a completed screen, a doctor's sign-off and emergency drugs at the chair.

            I'm scared of needles. Is this for me?
            Tell us when you book. A drip needs one small cannula in a vein in your arm, placed once. The doctor or nurse can talk you through it first, and you can sit back in the recliner while it goes in. If you decide on the day that it isn't for you, that's fine.
            What side effects can happen?
            The common ones are mild: a cool feeling in the arm, slight discomfort or a bruise where the cannula went in. Allergic reactions are rare, which is why the doctor screens your allergy history first and why emergency drugs and an anaphylaxis kit are at the chair for every session.
            Can I have a drip if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?
            Pregnancy is one of the flags in our screening. Tell the doctor at your consultation. The doctor decides whether any drip is appropriate, and the answer may be no.
            I have diabetes. Is that a problem?
            Tell us when you book. Fasting glucose or HbA1c is checked where relevant, because some ingredients, such as alpha-lipoic acid, can lower blood sugar. The doctor adjusts the plan or rules a drip out.
            I have kidney problems. Can I still have one?
            Everyone has a kidney function test (RFT) before a first drip. Kidney impairment is a screening flag, and some drips, such as high-dose vitamin C, may not be given. The doctor decides after seeing your results.
            How soon will I notice a difference?
            It varies from person to person. Some people say they notice something after the first session, some after a few, and some notice nothing. We don't promise a result.
            Do you do home visits?
            Yes. IV drips are available at our clinic and at home, with the same tests, prescription and protocol. Home-visit area: ask us about your area when you book.
            How do I pay?
            Payment details are confirmed when you book. Your consultation is free when you book any IV drip.
