Section 4 · D · new

The compounding bench

Your drip is made as you scroll: each vial lifts off the tray, tips over the bag and pours; the colour blooms in the liquid and its line prints on the label. The site’s signature move, the drip assembling itself.

Open D → Section 4 · E · new

The pharmacy wall

A wall of ingredient bins with the drips hanging below. Choose a drip and its bins slide out with their doses, a line down to the bag; point at a bin and every drip that takes it lifts.

The loupe

The drips lie on a lit table, too small to read. A pharmacist’s loupe follows your pointer (or finger) and magnifies the real printed label underneath. Reading the label is how you choose.

Section 3 (chosen: B, four roads)

A doctor, before a needle: three options

Open each one and scroll through it. They show the section on its own, without the sterile field before it; the one you choose is then built into the page and joined to it.

Option A

The physician’s desk

The same linen table, from above. Your drip’s own protocol is read down with a ruler, line by line, each line ticked in pen. Then a real rubber stamp comes down four times on a pad of decision slips, and each slip is torn off and laid on the protocol.

Open A → Option B

One decision, four roads

A clean ink diagram on chart paper. Your answers run down to the physician, and the scroll then travels each road they can send you down: declines (it ends), asks for more (it loops back to you), changes the doses and approves (both go on to the bag).

Open B → Option C

Brand-new concepts, shorter (about two screens)

Option D · new

Signed, by name

Your session report on the linen. The camera moves in on the physician’s block: their name and registration number are printed in, and the protocol is signed in ink. Then: and they can say no.

Open D → Option E · new

A doctor can say no

Type only, and bold. What the quiz screens for turns past a reading line, pregnancy, renal failure, cardiac failure, certain drug interactions, each marked “a physician declines”, until it stops on everyone else: approved, for 90 days.

Open E → Option F · new

Fifteen minutes

A phone on the table, on a call with your physician. Your scroll runs the call’s clock from 0:00 to 15:00 while your quiz answers are read through beside it. The call ends; the protocol is signed.
